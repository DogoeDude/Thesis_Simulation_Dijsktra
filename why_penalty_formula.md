# 🧮 Penalty Formula Documentation

This document explains the **Weighted Additive Penalty** system used in [MCASimulation](file:///c:/Users/Reji/Desktop/Thesis_Simulation_Dijsktra/Baseline/mca_base_penalty_simulation.py#31-1379) to model the impact of hazards on crowd movement.

## 1. Overview
The simulation uses a composite score to quantify the "danger" or "friction" of a road cell. This score influences both **Movement Speed** and **Pathfinding Cost**.

## 2. The 6 Penalty Components
Each hazard is assigned a **Multiplier (Weight)** that represents its relative severity.

| Hazard | Weight ($W$) | Code Variable | Logic Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fire** | **1.0** | `W_FIRE` | Propagation & Decay logic |
| **Smoke** | **0.8** | `W_SMOKE` | Generated by Fire |
| **Density** | **0.6** | `W_DENSITY` | Calculated: $\text{Current Density} / 3.5$ |
| **Debris** | **0.4** | `W_DEBRIS` | Static or Event-based |
| **Terrain** | **0.2** | `W_TERRAIN` | Static map attribute |
| **Temp Obj** | **0.2** | `W_TEMP_OBJ` | Dynamic obstacles |

---

## 3. How the Calculation Works (Step-by-Step)

### Step A: Normalization
For every cell, the simulation checks the hazard level (0.0 to 1.0).
**Example:** `Fire Level = 0.5`

### Step B: The Weighted Sum
Each hazard is multiplied by its weight and all are added together:
> **Total Score** = (Fire × 1.0) + (Smoke × 0.8) + (Density × 0.6) + (Debris × 0.4) + (Terrain × 0.2) + (Temp × 0.2)

### Step C: The 0.95 Cap
If the total score goes above 0.95, it is forced back down to 0.95.
> **Final Penalty** = The smaller of (0.95) OR (Total Score)

---

## 4. Impact on Simulation

### 🏃 Impact on Movement Speed
The penalty directly slows down agents. The computer calculates it like this:
> **Real Speed** = [Free Flow Speed] × [Crowd Factor] × (1.0 - Final Penalty)

*   **If Penalty is 0**: You move at 100% of your current potential speed.
*   **If Penalty is 0.95**: You move at only 5% of your potential speed (crawling).

### 🗺️ Impact on Dijkstra Pathfinding
In the Dijkstra scripts, hazards make a path look "longer" so agents avoid it:
> **Calculated Path Cost** = Actual Distance × (1.0 + Final Penalty × 50.0)

*   This means a cell with a **0.95 penalty** feels **48.5 times longer** than a clear cell, making agents "reroute" to safer (but physically longer) paths.

---

## 5. Rationale for Constant Values

### Why the 0.7 Reroute Threshold?
The value **0.7** is the "sweet spot" for balancing AI intelligence and performance:
1.  **Immediate Reaction to Danger**: Since Fire ($1.0$) and Smoke ($0.8$) are above $0.7$, agents react and reroute the *instant* these appear.
2.  **Filtering "Normal" Crowds**: A crowd alone provides a penalty of $0.6$. Because $0.6 < 0.7$, agents won't panic and constantly recalculate their paths just because a road is a bit busy. This keeps the simulation stable and fast.
3.  **Detecting Dangerous Bottlenecks**: If a Crowd ($0.6$) encounters even minor Debris ($0.2$), the total hits $0.8$. This triggers a reroute, effectively telling the AI: *"This specific crowd is now in danger—find another way."*

### Why the 0.95 Max Penalty?
This is a technical **Safety Net**:
-   **Prevents "Frozen" Simulations**: If the penalty hit $1.0$, speed would become $0$. Agents would become immovable objects, potentially breaking the movement logic or causing infinite loops.
-   **Realistic "Crawl"**: $0.95$ ensures that even in the worst disaster, agents still have **5% speed**. This allows them to "shove" through or "crawl" out of hazards rather than becoming statues.

## 6. Example Calculations

### Scenario A: Minor Smoke & Crowding
An agent is walking through a light crowd in a smoky area.
- Smoke Level: `0.3`
- Crowd Density: `2.0` (Density Score = $2.0 / 3.5 \approx 0.57$)
- **Calculation**: $(0.3 \times 0.8) + (0.57 \times 0.6) = 0.24 + 0.34 = \mathbf{0.58}$
- **Result**: Agent moves at 42% speed. No Dijkstra reroute triggered ($0.58 < 0.7$).

### Scenario B: Severe Fire (The "Wall")
A cell is engulfed in fire.
- Fire Level: `1.0`
- **Calculation**: $(1.0 \times 1.0) = \mathbf{1.0}$
- **Capped Result**: $\min(1.0, 0.95) = \mathbf{0.95}$
- **Result**: Agent crawls at 5% speed. Immediate Dijkstra reroute triggered ($0.95 > 0.7$).

### Scenario C: Cumulative "Chaos"
A crowded area ($0.6$) with debris ($0.3$) and terrain issues ($0.5$).
- Density: `3.5` (Score = $1.0$)
- Debris: `0.3`
- Terrain: `0.5`
- **Calculation**: $(1.0 \times 0.6) + (0.3 \times 0.4) + (0.5 \times 0.2) = 0.6 + 0.12 + 0.1 = \mathbf{0.82}$
- **Result**: Agent moves at 18% speed. Dijkstra reroute triggered ($0.82 > 0.7$).

### Scenario D: The "Everything" Scenario
A cell where literally every hazard is present at a moderate level.

| Hazard | Sample Intensity | Multiplier | Weighted Contribution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fire** | 0.2 (Start) | 1.0 | **0.20** |
| **Smoke** | 0.3 (Visible) | 0.8 | **0.24** |
| **Density** | 1.75 (Mid) | 0.6 | **0.30** |
| **Debris** | 0.2 (Clutter) | 0.4 | **0.08** |
| **Terrain** | 0.5 (Rubbles) | 0.2 | **0.10** |
| **Temp Obj**| 0.4 (Blocks) | 0.2 | **0.08** |
| **TOTAL** | | | **1.00** |

- **Final Calculation**: $\min(1.00, 0.95) = \mathbf{0.95}$
- **Result**: Even if everything is only at 20%-50%, the cumulative effect hits the **0.95 maximum**. Agents are reduced to crawl speed and the Dijkstra AI will desperately find a way around this area.

## 7. Defense & Academic Support

### Why use Multipliers (Weights)?
The simulation uses a **Multi-Criteria Analysis (MCA)** approach. In academic safety modeling, not all hazards are equal. Multipliers allow the system to prioritize life-threatening dangers (like Fire) over inconveniences (like Rough Terrain).

### Academic Justification for the Values:

1.  **Fire (1.0) - The Primary Threat**:
    - **Defense**: Fire is considered the "Untenable Limit." In fire safety engineering, direct exposure to flames or extreme heat is a binary barrier to survival.
    - **Study**: Standard *SFPE Handbook of Fire Protection Engineering* principles treat fire as the 100% priority hazard for evacuation routing.

2.  **Smoke (0.8) - The Visibility & Toxicity Barrier**:
    - **Defense**: Smoke is the leading cause of casualties in evacuations. It reduces walking speed significantly due to visibility loss and irritant effects.
    - **Study**: Research on *ASET (Available Safe Egress Time)* shows that smoke visibility often reduces movement speed to roughly 20%-30% of normal before it becomes lethal, justifying a high 0.8 weight.

3.  **Density (0.6) - The Physical Bottleneck**:
    - **Defense**: Crowd density follows the **Greenshields Model** or **Social Force Model**. It is a physical constraint that prevents movement but doesn't necessarily kill the agent immediately unless it leads to a stampede.
    - **Study**: Traditional *Mesoscopic Cellular Automata* papers (like the ones cited in your project) use density as the primary "friction" for flow conservation equations.

4.  **Debris/Terrain (0.4 - 0.2) - Passive Friction**:
    - **Defense**: These are "Static Hazards." They represent physical obstructions that slow people down but don't propagate or change state rapidly like fire or smoke.

### How to Defend this in a Thesis:
"This model utilizes a **Weighted Additive Model** consistent with **Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP)** methodologies. By assigning a hierarchy of $1.0$ to active lethal threats (Fire) and $0.2$ to passive environmental friction (Terrain), the simulation accurately reflects human decision-making: prioritizing survival from immediate danger while still accounting for physical bottlenecks."

## 8. Where do Intensities come from?

The "Intensity" of each hazard is not just a random number; it is generated by specific systems within the simulation:

| Hazard | Source of Intensity | How it changes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Density** | **State Variable** | Calculated every second as `Agents / Cell Area`. |
| **Fire** | **Propagation Logic** | Starts from a "seed" (event), Grows by `+0.03` per second, and has a `13%` chance to spread to neighbors. |
| **Smoke** | **Automated Logic** | Generated whenever Fire is present (starts at `0.4` intensity). It accumulates and decays naturally. |
| **Debris** | **Stochastic Events** | Triggered by `Random Collapse` events (0.5% chance per second) or manually set in a Disaster Scenario. |
| **Temp Obj**| **Linked Events** | Usually paired with Debris or specific route blockages. |
| **Terrain** | **Map Attributes** | Static value initialized to `0.0` (baseline). Can be pre-loaded from GeoPackage attributes if road quality varies. |
